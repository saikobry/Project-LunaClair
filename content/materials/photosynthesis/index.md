# Photosynthesis

## Overview

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It is an endergonic, anabolic pathway.

### General Equation

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**

### Site of Photosynthesis
- Occurs in the **chloroplasts** of plant cells

### Chloroplast Structure
- **Thylakoids**: Flattened sacs where light-dependent reactions occur
- **Granum**: Stack of thylakoids
- **Stroma**: Fluid-filled space surrounding the thylakoids (site of the Calvin cycle)
- **Chlorophyll**: Green pigment that absorbs light energy

### Two Major Stages
1. Light-Dependent Reactions
2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)

## Light-Dependent Reactions

### Location
- Thylakoid membranes

### Requirements
- Light energy
- Water (H₂O)
- NADP⁺
- ADP + Pi

### Key Events

#### Photosystem II (P680)
- Light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll
- Electrons are passed to the electron transport chain
- Water is split (photolysis) to replace lost electrons
- Oxygen (O₂) is released as a byproduct

#### Electron Transport Chain (Cytochrome Complex)
- High-energy electrons pass through protein complexes
- Energy released pumps H⁺ ions into the thylakoid lumen
- Creates a proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane

#### Photosystem I (P700)
- Light energy re-energizes the electrons
- Electrons are transferred to NADP⁺, forming NADPH

#### Chemiosmosis
- H⁺ ions flow back to the stroma through ATP synthase
- ATP synthase produces ATP from ADP + Pi

### Products
- **ATP** (from photophosphorylation)
- **NADPH** (reducing power)
- **O₂** (byproduct)

## Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)

### Location
- Stroma of the chloroplast

### Requirements
- CO₂ (from the atmosphere)
- ATP (from light-dependent reactions)
- NADPH (from light-dependent reactions)

### Phases of the Calvin Cycle

#### Carbon Fixation
- CO₂ is attached to RuBP (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate), a 5-carbon molecule
- Catalyzed by the enzyme **RuBisCO**
- Forms an unstable 6-carbon intermediate that splits into two 3-PGA molecules

#### Reduction Phase
- 3-PGA is phosphorylated by ATP
- NADPH reduces the molecule to G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate)
- G3P is a 3-carbon sugar

#### Regeneration of RuBP
- Most G3P molecules are used to regenerate RuBP
- Requires additional ATP
- Allows the cycle to continue

### Net Output
- For every 3 CO₂ molecules fixed: **1 G3P** (net gain)
- Takes 3 turns of the cycle to produce one G3P

## Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

### Light Intensity
- Increasing light intensity increases the rate of photosynthesis
- Until a saturation point is reached

### Carbon Dioxide Concentration
- Higher CO₂ levels increase photosynthetic rate
- Until other factors become limiting

### Temperature
- Enzymes (especially RuBisCO) work optimally at specific temperatures
- Too high or too low temperatures reduce efficiency
- Optimal range: 25–30°C for most plants

### Water Availability
- Water stress causes stomata to close
- Reduces CO₂ intake and slows photosynthesis

## Comparison: Photosynthesis vs Cellular Respiration

| Feature | Photosynthesis | Cellular Respiration |
|---------|---------------|---------------------|
| Energy type | Endergonic (stores energy) | Exergonic (releases energy) |
| Reactants | CO₂ + H₂O + Light | Glucose + O₂ |
| Products | Glucose + O₂ | CO₂ + H₂O + ATP |
| Location | Chloroplasts | Mitochondria |
| Occurrence | Plants, algae, some bacteria | All living organisms |
