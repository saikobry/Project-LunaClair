# Genetics & Heredity

## Introduction to Genetics

Genetics is the study of heredity and variation in living organisms. It explains how traits are passed from parents to offspring through genes.

### Key Terminology
- **Gene**: A unit of heredity made of DNA
- **Allele**: A variant form of a gene
- **Genotype**: The genetic makeup of an organism
- **Phenotype**: The observable characteristics of an organism
- **Homozygous**: Having two identical alleles for a trait
- **Heterozygous**: Having two different alleles for a trait
- **Dominant**: An allele that masks the expression of another allele
- **Recessive**: An allele whose expression is masked by a dominant allele

## Mendelian Genetics

### Gregor Mendel (1822–1884)
- Austrian monk considered the father of genetics
- Experimented with pea plants (Pisum sativum)
- Studied seven distinct traits with clear dominant/recessive patterns

### Mendel's Laws

#### Law of Segregation
- Each individual has two alleles for each trait
- During gamete formation, alleles separate randomly
- Each gamete carries only one allele for each trait

#### Law of Independent Assortment
- Genes for different traits assort independently during gamete formation
- Applies to genes located on different chromosomes
- Explains genetic variation in offspring

#### Law of Dominance
- In a heterozygote, one allele may be dominant over the other
- The dominant allele determines the phenotype

### Punnett Squares
- A grid used to predict the genotypes of offspring
- Monohybrid cross: 4 squares (2 × 2)
- Dihybrid cross: 16 squares (4 × 4)

### Typical Ratios
- **Monohybrid cross (heterozygous × heterozygous)**: 3:1 phenotypic ratio
- **Dihybrid cross (heterozygous × heterozygous)**: 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio

## Beyond Mendelian Genetics

### Incomplete Dominance
- Neither allele is completely dominant
- Heterozygous phenotype is intermediate
- Example: Snapdragon flower color (red + white = pink)

### Codominance
- Both alleles are fully expressed in the heterozygote
- Example: ABO blood type — both A and B antigens are expressed

### Multiple Alleles
- More than two alleles exist for a gene in a population
- Example: ABO blood groups (Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i)

### Polygenic Inheritance
- Multiple genes contribute to a single trait
- Results in continuous variation
- Examples: Height, skin color, eye color

### Pleiotropy
- A single gene influences multiple phenotypic traits
- Example: Sickle cell anemia affects many body systems

## DNA Structure and Replication

### DNA Structure (Watson and Crick, 1953)
- Double helix structure
- Composed of nucleotides
- Each nucleotide contains: phosphate group, deoxyribose sugar, nitrogenous base

### Nitrogenous Bases
- **Purines**: Adenine (A), Guanine (G)
- **Pyrimidines**: Thymine (T), Cytosine (C)

### Base Pairing Rules
- A pairs with T (2 hydrogen bonds)
- G pairs with C (3 hydrogen bonds)

### DNA Replication
- **Semiconservative**: Each new DNA molecule has one old strand and one new strand
- **Enzymes involved**:
  - **Helicase**: Unwinds the DNA double helix
  - **DNA polymerase**: Synthesizes new DNA strands
  - **Primase**: Lays down RNA primers
  - **Ligase**: Seals gaps between Okazaki fragments

## Protein Synthesis

### Central Dogma of Molecular Biology
**DNA → RNA → Protein**

#### Transcription (DNA → mRNA)
- Occurs in the nucleus
- RNA polymerase synthesizes mRNA from a DNA template
- mRNA carries the genetic code to ribosomes

#### Translation (mRNA → Protein)
- Occurs at ribosomes in the cytoplasm
- mRNA codons specify amino acids
- tRNA brings amino acids to the ribosome
- Amino acids are linked to form a polypeptide chain

### Genetic Code
- Codons are triplets of nucleotides
- 64 possible codons (4³)
- 61 code for amino acids
- 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA)
- AUG is the start codon (codes for methionine)

## Mutations

### Types of Mutations

#### Point Mutations
- **Substitution**: One base is replaced by another
- **Silent mutation**: No change in amino acid
- **Missense mutation**: Change in amino acid
- **Nonsense mutation**: Premature stop codon

#### Frameshift Mutations
- **Insertion**: Extra base added
- **Deletion**: Base removed
- Both shift the reading frame, altering all subsequent codons

### Causes of Mutations
- **Spontaneous**: Errors during DNA replication
- **Induced**: Caused by mutagens (radiation, chemicals, viruses)
