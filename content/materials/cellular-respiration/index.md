# Cellular Respiration

## Overview

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate). It is an exergonic, catabolic pathway that releases energy from organic molecules.

### General Equation

**C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (ATP)**

### Stages of Cellular Respiration

1. Glycolysis
2. Pyruvate Oxidation
3. Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)
4. Electron Transport Chain & Oxidative Phosphorylation

## Glycolysis

### Location
- Occurs in the cytoplasm

### Key Events
- Glucose (6 carbons) is split into two molecules of pyruvate (3 carbons each)
- Occurs in ten enzymatic steps
- Does not require oxygen (anaerobic)

### Energy Investment Phase
- 2 ATP molecules are consumed
- Glucose is phosphorylated to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate

### Energy Payoff Phase
- 4 ATP molecules are produced (substrate-level phosphorylation)
- 2 NAD⁺ are reduced to 2 NADH

### Net Yield
- **2 ATP** (net gain)
- **2 NADH**
- **2 Pyruvate**

## Pyruvate Oxidation

### Location
- Mitochondrial matrix

### Key Events
- Pyruvate is transported into the mitochondria
- Each pyruvate is oxidized to acetyl CoA

### Products (per pyruvate)
- **1 CO₂** (released as waste)
- **1 NADH**
- **1 Acetyl CoA**

## Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)

### Location
- Mitochondrial matrix

### Key Events
- Acetyl CoA (2 carbons) combines with oxaloacetate (4 carbons) to form citrate (6 carbons)
- Through a series of oxidation reactions, citrate is converted back to oxaloacetate
- NAD⁺ and FAD are reduced

### Products (per acetyl CoA)
- **2 CO₂**
- **3 NADH**
- **1 FADH₂**
- **1 ATP** (by substrate-level phosphorylation)

## Electron Transport Chain & Oxidative Phosphorylation

### Location
- Inner mitochondrial membrane

### Electron Transport Chain (ETC)
- A series of protein complexes (I–IV) embedded in the inner membrane
- NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the ETC
- Electrons are passed through the complexes, releasing energy
- Oxygen serves as the final electron acceptor

### Chemiosmosis
- Energy from electron flow pumps H⁺ ions into the intermembrane space
- Creates a proton gradient (electrochemical gradient)
- H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase
- ATP synthase uses this flow to produce ATP

### ATP Yield
- Each NADH yields approximately **2.5 ATP**
- Each FADH₂ yields approximately **1.5 ATP**

## Total ATP Yield

| Stage | ATP (per glucose) |
|-------|-------------------|
| Glycolysis | 2 ATP |
| Pyruvate Oxidation | 0 ATP |
| Krebs Cycle | 2 ATP |
| Electron Transport Chain | ~28 ATP |
| **Total** | **~32 ATP** |

## Anaerobic Respiration

### Fermentation
- Occurs when oxygen is not available
- Produces only 2 ATP per glucose (from glycolysis only)

#### Lactic Acid Fermentation
- Pyruvate is reduced to lactate
- NADH is oxidized to NAD⁺
- Occurs in muscle cells during intense exercise

#### Alcoholic Fermentation
- Pyruvate is converted to ethanol and CO₂
- NADH is oxidized to NAD⁺
- Occurs in yeast and some bacteria
- Used in brewing, winemaking, and baking

## Regulation of Cellular Respiration

### Feedback Inhibition
- High ATP levels inhibit glycolysis (phosphofructokinase)
- High citrate levels inhibit glycolysis
- High NADH levels inhibit the Krebs cycle

### Hormonal Control
- Insulin promotes glucose uptake and glycolysis
- Glucagon promotes glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis
